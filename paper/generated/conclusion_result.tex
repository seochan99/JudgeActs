A 4B VLM judge that chooses which image to return barely beats random choice (0.039), falls short of a CLIP similarity scorer, and picks the first-listed image in 48.8\% of calls. Agreement across orders separates its good decisions from its bad ones; agreement with a weaker judge that shares its position bias does not. A larger 8B judge from the same family shows almost no position bias, and for it the same order-agreement gate mostly discards good decisions; a gate validated for one judge is not validated for the next. Agreement is useful only when it targets the failure mode of the judge in front of it, so selection audits, including audits of the filters around a judge, must be repeated when the judge changes.

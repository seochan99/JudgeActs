Table~\ref{tab:main} and Figure~\ref{fig:results}a summarize the direct policies. Qwen returns a valid original-order choice for all 300 prompts. Its mean alignment is 0.770, against 0.730 for random choice and 0.929 for the oracle, a gain of 0.039 (95\% CI [0.016, 0.062]). Mean regret is 0.160 [0.136, 0.184]; Qwen returns a best-rated image for 51.0\% of prompts and a below-mean image for 36.3\%, against 43.1\% for random choice (paired difference $-$0.067, 95\% CI [$-$0.118, $-$0.016]).

Smol does worse than random: gain $-$0.073 [$-$0.093, $-$0.052], regret 0.270, and BMR 60.3\%. It rarely follows the output format (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:validity}), and its interface and image resolution differ from Qwen's, so we treat it as a weak secondary judge rather than as a capability comparison. Appendix Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity} shows that the conclusions hold with a below-mean margin of 0.05, with the median in place of the mean, and with overall satisfaction as the utility.
